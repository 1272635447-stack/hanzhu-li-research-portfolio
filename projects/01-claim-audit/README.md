# Project 01 — AI-Generated Research-Claim Audit

## Overview

This work began with an audit of **40 claims across five AI-generated research cards**, checking each claim against its cited research paper. The review recorded method-description misdescriptions, terminology shifts, and omitted qualifications. These observations were recorded during a researcher-led review and were not treated as quantified error categories.

The audit motivated a claim-level approach: rather than asking only whether a cited paper is relevant, inspect which parts of the source support, qualify, contradict, or fail to establish each component of a particular claim.

## Work completed

- Reviewed 40 AI-generated claims across five research cards against cited source papers.
- Recorded the supporting passage and a written rationale for each claim.
- Noted two claims that repeated the same methodological misdescription: the source paper described one-to-one semi-structured video interviews, while randomisation applied to the order in which participants encountered five prototypes.
- Also recorded terminology shifts and omitted qualifications in other claims, without assigning unverified category counts.
- Organised source-linked materials covering 12 cases and 23 subclaims.
- Used four provisional substantive judgement categories: Supported, Supported with caveat, Contradicted, and Not established. **Pending** is a workflow status for unresolved evidence or adjudication, not a fifth substantive judgement category.

## Interpretation and limitations

This is a researcher-led, single-reviewer audit and materials-development exercise. It was not a study of students, was not designed to estimate the prevalence or rate of AI errors, and should not be represented as independent expert consensus or a contemporaneous independent blind review. Any retrospective AI-assisted reconstruction is identified as such.

The judgement categories are provisional working labels. They have not been validated as a student assessment or psychometric scale. A study involving student responses would require a written rubric, independent coding by at least two trained raters, and a documented process for resolving disagreements.
